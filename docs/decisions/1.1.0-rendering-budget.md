# v1.1.0 Rendering Budget Decision

Status: recommended for v1.1.0

## Scope

This decision covers the next low-poly environmental object pass for Aeolian Wilds. It is intentionally scoped to procedural props and rendering budgets, not gameplay systems or imported art pipelines.

The current renderer already has the right foundation:

- Terrain streams deterministic chunks around the player and disposes distant chunks.
- Terrain uses indexed `BufferGeometry`, world-space gradient normals, vertex colors, and one shared `MeshLambertMaterial`.
- Foliage uses shared geometries and `InstancedMesh` batches for trunks, canopies, and bushes.
- Water is a recentered opaque `MeshPhongMaterial` plane.
- The debug snapshot reports FPS, frame time, chunks, foliage counts, renderer calls, triangles, geometry and texture counts, heap, terrain geometry MB, and chunk build time.

## Decision

For v1.1.0, add environmental richness through deterministic, low-poly, instanced object families before increasing terrain radius or importing GLB props.

Recommended object families:

- Shoreline stones, driftwood, reeds, and sparse shell-like accent rocks.
- Grassland and forest ground clutter such as small stones, fallen logs, stumps, and low shrubs.
- Desert cacti, dry brush, dune stones, and wind-worn log shapes.
- Mountain scree, larger angular boulders, snowline rocks, and sparse dead snags.

Each placement must be a pure function of world `x,z`, chunk coordinate, and seed-derived salts. Use the existing `heightAt`, `normalAt`, `slopeAt`, `moistureAt`, and biome classification functions to reject underwater, steep, or biome-inappropriate placements. Do not introduce saved placement maps.

The first v1.1.0 pass should favor small and medium props that improve ground readability from the third-person camera. Large landmark props are allowed, but they should be rare, deterministic, and still use shared geometry or pooled meshes.

## Instancing And Geometry

Use one system-level batch per object family, geometry variant, material, and LOD band across the loaded chunk ring. Do not create one `Mesh` per prop, and do not create one full set of instanced meshes per terrain chunk unless measurement proves ring-level batches are worse.

Recommended pattern:

- Collect placements from the current loaded chunk coordinate signature, similar to `FoliageSystem`.
- Sort or cap deterministically so the closest and most biome-relevant props survive when caps are hit.
- Fill `InstancedMesh` matrices and optional instance colors in one pass.
- Set `instanceMatrix.needsUpdate` and `instanceColor.needsUpdate` after the batch is filled.
- Recompute instanced bounds after count changes.
- Keep matrices static during ordinary gameplay. If wind or shimmer is added later, prefer shader-side phase and amplitude over per-frame matrix writes.

Geometry choices:

- Use shared low-segment primitives first: dodecahedron or icosahedron rocks, 5- or 6-sided cylinders for logs and trunks, cones for reeds or cacti, flattened dodecahedrons for shrubs, and simple indexed custom geometry only when primitives cannot express the silhouette.
- Prefer vertex color and `instanceColor` variation over more materials or textures.
- Keep materials opaque and fog-enabled. Use `MeshLambertMaterial` for most props; use `MeshPhongMaterial` only where specular response materially improves readability.
- Avoid realtime shadows for decorative props in v1.1.0.
- Avoid alpha-blended grass cards. If alpha is required, use alpha test and keep it rare because water, clouds, sprites, and alpha cards add fill-rate and sorting pressure.
- Keep collision proxies separate from render meshes. Most decorative props should have no collision; gameplay blockers should use simple circle, capsule, or box proxies in layout/gameplay data.

Suggested initial caps:

- Small stones and scree: 1200 to 1800 instances.
- Reeds, cacti, dry brush, and shrubs: 700 to 1200 instances total beyond existing bushes.
- Logs, stumps, and dead snags: 150 to 300 instances.
- Large boulders and landmark accents: 60 to 120 instances.

These are not guaranteed budgets; they are starting caps. The debug overlay and smoke tests should decide the final values.

## Terrain Triangle And Memory Guidance

Do not increase `TERRAIN_RADIUS` for the first environmental-object pass. Radius is expensive because each additional terrain chunk is another draw call, another geometry allocation, and more placement work.

If terrain detail needs a v1.1.0 bump, increase `CHUNK_SEGMENTS` one step at a time and keep the radius stable:

| `CHUNK_SEGMENTS` | Triangles per chunk | Approx terrain triangles at 61 chunks | Approx terrain geometry |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 12 current | 288 | 17.6k | 0.6 MB |
| 16 | 512 | 31.2k | 1.0 MB |
| 20 | 800 | 48.8k | 1.5 MB |

The current hard smoke limit is `terrainGeometryMB < 8`, but v1.1.0 should target `terrainGeometryMB < 2` so future biome, town, and gameplay systems have room.

Use this order when spending budget:

1. Add instanced environmental props while keeping `CHUNK_SEGMENTS = 12` and `TERRAIN_RADIUS = 4`.
2. If terrain faceting is still the dominant visual issue, test `CHUNK_SEGMENTS = 16`.
3. Consider `CHUNK_SEGMENTS = 20` only if total triangles remain comfortably below 150k and chunk build time stays below the smoke contract.
4. Avoid `TERRAIN_RADIUS = 5` unless fog, camera, and exploration readability require it. A radius bump adds many terrain draw calls and should be treated as a separate rendering decision.

## WebGL And WebGPU

Keep `WebGLRenderer` as the v1.1.0 baseline. WebGPU should not be required to hit the v1.1.0 object budget.

WebGL guidance:

- Preserve the current `antialias: false` renderer setting and pixel ratio cap unless visual QA proves a change is worth the cost.
- Treat draw calls and transparent overdraw as primary constraints.
- Keep materials simple and shared.
- Track `renderer.info.render.calls`, `renderer.info.render.triangles`, `renderer.info.memory.geometries`, and `renderer.info.memory.textures`, but treat them as trend counters rather than complete memory truth.
- Add app-owned counters for environmental object instance counts and estimated instance buffer bytes.
- Dispose chunk-owned and system-owned GPU resources explicitly when a system is torn down. Do not dispose shared global resources without clear ownership.

WebGPU guidance:

- WebGPU can be explored later for TSL materials, compute culling, or very high instance counts, but `navigator.gpu.requestAdapter()` can fail and support still needs fallback handling.
- Avoid v1.1.0 effects that depend on WebGL-only `onBeforeCompile` paths if an equivalent standard material, custom `ShaderMaterial`, or future node/TSL path is reasonable.
- Keep placement, capping, and LOD logic renderer-agnostic so a future WebGPU path can reuse the same world data.

## Budget Targets

The existing project budgets remain the hard contract:

- Visible-browser target: 60 FPS.
- Chromium headless smoke floor: greater than 45 FPS.
- JS heap: under 100 MB when `performance.memory` is available.
- Exploration render calls: under 145.
- Triangles: under 150k for the default camera and terrain radius.
- Textures: under 12.
- Geometries: under 120.
- Terrain geometry: under 8 MB.
- Chunk build time: under 12 ms in smoke.

Recommended v1.1.0 working targets:

- Render calls: target 125 to 130, hard stop at 145.
- Additional environmental object draw calls: target 8 to 14, hard stop at 24.
- Total triangles: target under 125k, hard stop at 150k.
- Environmental object triangles: target under 45k at the default camera.
- Geometries: target under 90, hard stop at 120.
- Textures: target 8 or fewer, hard stop at 12. Prefer zero new textures for v1.1.0 props.
- Terrain geometry: target under 2 MB.
- Object instance buffer estimate: target under 1 MB.
- Chunk build time: target under 8 ms, hard stop at 12 ms.
- Object placement rebuild: target under 2.5 ms when the loaded chunk signature changes.

If a screenshot looks substantially better but crosses a hard budget, the feature needs an explicit follow-up decision instead of quietly raising the limit.

## Required Debug Additions

When the environmental object system lands, extend `window.__OPEN_WORLD_DEBUG__.getSnapshot()` with:

- Environmental object total instance count.
- Counts by family or biome family.
- Environmental object draw-call contribution if easy to derive, or the number of active instanced batches.
- Estimated instance buffer MB.
- Placement rebuild time for the last sync.
- Any asset or procedural generation errors.

Keep the existing overlay readable; add only the most useful aggregate lines to the on-screen text.

## Concrete Tests

Run the existing handoff checks for the v1.1.0 implementation:

```bash
npm run build
npm test
npm run test:smoke
npm run capture:screenshots
```

Add unit coverage for object placement:

- Deterministic placement for the same chunk coordinates and seed.
- Stable caps when chunk order changes.
- No placements below `SEA_LEVEL` unless the family is explicitly shoreline/water-edge and still visually above the water plane.
- Slope rejection for trees, cacti, logs, and large props.
- Biome filters produce expected object families across shore, desert, forest, grassland, rock, and snow samples.

Extend smoke coverage:

- Assert the debug snapshot includes environmental object counts and placement timing.
- Assert object counts are greater than zero after the default world has streamed.
- Assert counts remain below caps.
- Assert calls, triangles, geometries, textures, heap, terrain geometry MB, chunk build time, and FPS remain inside the hard budgets.
- Move the player to at least one dry biome target and one shoreline or rocky target, wait for streaming, and assert the budget still holds.
- Assert console and page errors remain empty.
- If teardown or scene reset exists later, assert geometry and texture counts do not grow after repeated transitions.

## Screenshot Checks

Capture v1.1.0 screenshots under `artifacts/screenshots/v1.1.0/`.

Minimum set:

- `01-title.png`: title screen still renders cleanly.
- `02-world-debug.png`: default spawned world with debug overlay visible.
- `03-streamed-biome.png`: existing streamed dry biome check.
- `04-shoreline-detail.png`: player near a shore with water-edge props visible.
- `05-forest-or-grassland-detail.png`: dense but readable vegetation and ground clutter.
- `06-rocky-slope-detail.png`: boulders or scree on elevated terrain without slope violations.

Visual acceptance checks:

- Canvas is nonblank and correctly framed.
- Debug overlay text does not overlap or become unreadable.
- Props are not floating, buried, or visibly z-fighting with terrain.
- Props do not spawn in open water unless intentionally designed for the shoreline band.
- Object scale reads correctly from the gameplay camera.
- Foliage and new props do not hide the player at normal camera distance.
- Transparent clouds, sun sprite, water, and any alpha-tested props do not show obvious sorting artifacts.
- The scene remains visually low-poly and coherent with the existing terrain, foliage, water, and sky.
- Visible-browser QA holds near 60 FPS while rotating the camera and moving through a dense prop area.

## Recommendation Summary

Ship v1.1.0 environmental detail as a procedural instanced-object system with shared low-poly geometry, vertex/instance color variation, strict caps, and debug-visible counts. Keep terrain radius fixed. Only raise terrain segments after object budgets are measured, and prefer `CHUNK_SEGMENTS = 16` as the first terrain-quality experiment.
