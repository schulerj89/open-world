# v1.1.0 Environment Direction

## Decision

Make the low-poly endless world more fun by turning wandering into a chain of readable environmental promises:

1. The player sees a strong silhouette, color break, or terrain feature within the current fog range.
2. The route to it asks for a small choice: ridge, shore, forest gap, dune trough, or safer lowland.
3. Arrival reveals a compact micro-destination with a distinct shape language, better vista, or next visible hook.

This should stay an exploration-first environment update, not a combat, quest, inventory, or high-fidelity asset update. The v1.1.0 goal is to make movement feel intentional every 30 to 90 seconds while preserving the current clean procedural engine.

## Why This Fits The Current Engine

The engine is already well suited to sparse, procedural attraction points:

- Terrain, moisture, biomes, foliage density, and spawn selection are deterministic functions of world `x,z` plus seed. Landmark and micro-destination placement can use the same model without saved maps.
- `TerrainSystem` streams a circular footprint of 64 meter chunks, sorted nearest-first inside a 4.8 ms frame build budget. Environment additions should attach to loaded chunk coordinates and rebuild only when the loaded-chunk signature changes.
- The world is low-poly, vertex-colored, fogged, and horizon-readable. Large simple silhouettes will read better than dense prop clutter.
- `FoliageSystem` already proves the right rendering pattern: shared geometries, shared materials, deterministic placements, and `InstancedMesh` batches.
- `WaterSystem`, `SkySystem`, and `CloudSystem` are recentered around the player. v1.1.0 can add local visual variety without changing the endless world architecture.
- `window.__OPEN_WORLD_DEBUG__.getSnapshot()` already exposes FPS, chunk pressure, renderer calls, triangles, geometry and texture counts, heap, terrain bytes, player position, biome, and water-blocking. New environment systems can extend that snapshot with their own live counts.

## Game Inspirations

- `The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild`: copy the promise of visible destinations, hilltop reveals, and player-led route choice. Do not copy dense physics simulation or expensive systemic interactions in v1.1.0.
- `Sable`: copy the calm low-poly silhouettes, desert readability, and wide negative space. The current dunes, shores, and fog can support this style with very few assets.
- `A Short Hike`: copy compact navigation loops where nearby peaks, paths, and lookouts constantly suggest the next destination.
- `Journey`: copy dune crest reveals, strong horizon shapes, and quiet environmental payoff after a climb.
- `Valheim`: copy biome identity through distinct prop sets, color temperature, and readable transitions. Avoid adding crafting or resource simulation for this version.
- `Proteus`: copy procedural mood vignettes and musical wandering energy, especially small discoverable clearings and visual events that do not require heavy interaction code.

## Recommended v1.1.0 Environment Additions

### 1. Landmark Grammar

Add a deterministic landmark layer with one major landmark every 3 to 5 chunks and minor markers every 1 to 2 chunks. Use biome, elevation, slope, moisture, and seeded noise to choose from a small grammar:

- Shore: rock arcs, tide shelves, sand bars, bright shell-stone clusters.
- Grassland: wind stones, low flower meadows made from opaque low-poly instances, isolated lookout trees.
- Forest: clearings, mossy stone circles, fallen log bridges as simple cylinders, denser edge foliage around open centers.
- Desert: half-buried pillars, dune ribs, sun-bleached stone rows, mesa crowns.
- Rock and snow: cairn lines, needle rocks, ice-blue crystal clusters, cliff-top markers.

Major landmarks should be visible inside the current fog and chunk radius, not beyond it. With `TERRAIN_RADIUS = 4` and `FOG_FAR` at roughly 285 meters, the first version should favor silhouettes in the 120 to 260 meter range.

### 2. Spawn Basin With Three Hooks

Guarantee that the initial spawn area has three visible reasons to move:

- A close hook within one chunk, such as a clearing, cairn, or shore pocket.
- A medium hook near the fog midpoint, such as an arch, ridge marker, or grove edge.
- A horizon hook near the loaded-ring edge, such as a mesa crown, snow needle, or high lookout.

This can be deterministic from the chosen spawn point and current world fields. It should not require handcrafted terrain or a saved local map.

### 3. Traversal Affordances

Use environment shapes to make route decisions legible without new UI:

- Cairn lines and stone rows point through safe slope corridors.
- Grass color bands and lower bush density imply walkable forest gaps.
- Dune troughs and dry washes guide movement through desert without carved river systems.
- Shore shelves and rock arcs give coastlines places to follow and inspect.
- Ridge saddles and lookout stones help players find mountain passes while respecting the current slope rejection.

These additions should be visual and deterministic first. Avoid adding full trail meshes until the placement grammar proves fun.

### 4. Biome Edge Moments

Make biome transitions more memorable by placing sparse transition props where two conditions meet:

- Wet grass to forest: widening clearings with clustered canopy edges.
- Grass to desert: broken stone rows and thinning foliage.
- Shore to grass: sand shelves with grass tufts and drift-stone markers.
- Rock to snow: cairns, blue crystals, and stronger fog contrast.

The intent is to make the player notice that they crossed into a new region before the debug overlay names the biome.

### 5. Vista And Reveal Sites

Add small elevated vista sites on low-slope shoulders and ridge saddles. Each site should frame one other landmark or biome edge within the visible ring. A vista can be only a few stones, a flattened color patch, and an open foliage mask; it does not need a building.

### 6. Ambient Motion Without Simulation

Add low-cost motion only where it improves readability:

- Shader-side or material-color wind shimmer for grassland and forest batches.
- Very slow hue or brightness variation on crystals, sunlit stones, or shore markers.
- Cloud and fog tuning that frames major landmarks instead of hiding them.

Do not add per-instance matrix animation, particle-heavy weather, realtime shadows, or postprocessing for v1.1.0.

## Constraints To Preserve 60 FPS And 100 MB

Keep the repo budgets from `AGENTS.md` as hard acceptance criteria:

- Visible-browser target: 60 FPS.
- Headless smoke lower bound: 45 FPS.
- JS heap target: under 100 MB when `performance.memory` is available.
- Exploration render calls: under 145.
- Triangles: under 150k for default camera and terrain radius.
- Textures: under 12 unless a later feature explicitly raises the budget.
- Terrain geometry estimate: under 8 MB.
- Per-frame chunk build time in smoke: under 12 ms.

For v1.1.0 environment content, use these additional guardrails:

- Do not increase `TERRAIN_RADIUS`, `CHUNK_SEGMENTS`, camera far plane, or fog distance as part of the first environment pass.
- Add no more than 10 to 14 render calls for the whole environment layer. Prefer global or ring-level `InstancedMesh` batches by prop family and material over per-chunk mesh sets.
- Reserve no more than about 20k additional visible triangles for landmarks, markers, and biome props in the default view.
- Prefer opaque `MeshLambertMaterial` or `MeshPhongMaterial` with vertex colors. Avoid transparent grass cards, blended particles, and PBR materials.
- Reuse a small set of procedural geometries. Use generated canvas textures sparingly and keep the total texture count below the existing smoke limit.
- Keep placement deterministic from world coordinates and seed. Do not introduce saved terrain maps, random runtime placement drift, or network-loaded layout data.
- Recompute environment instance matrices only when loaded chunks change, not every frame.
- Dispose chunk-owned geometry, materials, and textures explicitly if any are owned by a streamed environment chunk. Shared global geometries and materials need clear ownership and should not be disposed by chunk teardown.
- Track app-owned environment memory: geometry bytes, instance matrix bytes, live landmark counts, live marker counts, and any texture estimates.
- Extend smoke tests or debug snapshots before raising density. At minimum, expose environment calls, triangles, instance counts, and estimated MB so budget regressions are visible.

## Non-Goals For v1.1.0

- No imported high-poly asset packs.
- No realtime shadows.
- No postprocessing stack.
- No rivers or dynamic watercourses beyond the current sea-level plane.
- No per-object collision for every marker. Use simple traversal hints first, and add collision only for major landmarks that need it.
- No quest system, inventory rewards, or combat encounters as dependencies for making the environment fun.

## Suggested Implementation Order

1. Add deterministic landmark scoring and debug-only markers for candidate points.
2. Add one shared instanced prop family for major silhouettes and one for minor cairns or stones.
3. Add biome-specific color and geometry variants while keeping the same instance batches.
4. Add spawn-basin hook selection and vista-site placement.
5. Add debug counters and smoke assertions before increasing density.
6. Capture v1.1.0 screenshots for title, debug overlay, biome transition, and one vista reveal.
