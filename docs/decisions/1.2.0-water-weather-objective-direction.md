# v1.2.0 Water, Weather, And Objective Direction

Status: accepted for v1.2.0

## Scope

This decision covers the next exploration pass for Aeolian Wilds: weather environments, water rendering and interaction, and a first objective loop. It does not choose a high-fidelity fluid simulation, combat system, inventory system, or imported art pipeline.

The current project already has the right base for this version:

- Deterministic terrain, biome, foliage, and environment placement from world `x,z` plus seed.
- `TERRAIN_RADIUS = 4`, v1.1 `CHUNK_SEGMENTS = 20`, fogged low-poly terrain, and streamed chunk disposal.
- `WaterSystem` as a recentered sea-level `MeshPhongMaterial` plane with water-blocking gameplay.
- `SkySystem`, `CloudSystem`, lights, fog, and debug instrumentation.
- Instanced environmental props and existing v1.1.0 exploration landmarks.

## Water And Fluid Options

| Option | Fit For v1.2.0 | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current custom `MeshPhongMaterial` water plane | Cheapest option: one recentered mesh, no render target, no dependency beyond `three`, fog-compatible, stable in WebGL. It fits a low-poly world where water is a readable traversal boundary more than a physical fluid. | Keep as the default path. Improve first with a procedural normal map, color, and shininess before replacing it. |
| `three/addons/objects/Water.js` | Official Three.js WebGL addon for flat reflective water. It supports normal-map distortion and render-target reflections, but texture size and reflection clarity directly affect cost. | Use only as a measured optional upgrade if the custom plane cannot sell major lakes or ocean vistas. Start at 256 or 512 render targets, one recentered plane, fog enabled, and no postprocessing. |
| `three/addons/objects/WaterMesh.js` | Official WebGPU water mesh path. It is not usable with the current `WebGLRenderer` baseline. | Defer. Keep v1.2.0 renderer-agnostic enough that this can be tested later behind a WebGPU capability path. |
| `Reflector` / `Refractor` addons | Useful for special flat reflective or refractive surfaces, but each render target adds GPU memory and extra render work. | Do not use for general endless water. Consider later for rare authored-feeling pools or caves only after budget proof. |
| Three.js GPGPU water and ocean shader examples | Good references for disturbances, smoothing, and wave language, but they are examples rather than a small drop-in library for this endless low-poly world. | Use as reference material, not as a v1.2.0 dependency. Avoid simulation textures and per-frame compute-style water in this pass. |
| Third-party fluid simulations | Usually solve 2D screen-space fluid, physics, or visual demos rather than endless terrain water boundaries. They add integration risk and are harder to keep deterministic across streamed chunks. | Reject for v1.2.0. The game needs readable water interaction, not fluid mechanics. |

## Decision

Ship v1.2.0 as an exploration-readable weather and water pass with a simple objective loop:

1. Add a `WeatherSystem` that drives sky color, fog, cloud density, light color, and precipitation from deterministic weather cells.
2. Add weather types that change navigation mood without hiding the world: clear, cloudy, rain, and snow. Wind is represented through precipitation drift and moving clouds.
3. Improve water as a traversal and landmark system with a scrolling procedural normal map on the existing `MeshPhongMaterial` sea plane, preserving the non-PBR light-based water path.
4. Add the first objective loop: find and attune three visible weather beacons within the exploration range. Each attuned point reveals the next target through the HUD and in-world beacon placement.
5. Keep the loop world-first. The target must be visible through silhouette, color, light, or weather clearing, not only through HUD text.

The objective should be deterministic from seed and local chunk coordinates. A new run can place different targets, but the same seed and start area should produce the same first expedition.

## Why This Fits The World

Weather is a high-leverage addition because the engine already has sky, clouds, fog, lighting, water, biome color, and debug counters. Most of the perceived change can come from material colors, fog ranges, cloud density, and a small number of instanced accents instead of heavy assets.

Water should remain stylized because the world is low-poly, fogged, and viewed from a third-person exploration camera. A fully simulated fluid surface would compete with the art direction, increase transparent overdraw, and spend GPU work on details the player is not asked to manipulate. For v1.2.0, water rejection plus readable shimmer is enough interaction; shore foam and wake feedback can follow after the objective/weather loop is stable.

The first objective loop gives wandering a purpose without adding large systems. Three nearby targets create a 10 to 20 minute expedition structure, test weather readability across biomes, and give screenshots a concrete before/after story. The loop can reuse existing landmarks, instanced geometry, and debug instrumentation.

## Game Inspirations

- `The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild`: use visible destination promises, weather as route pressure, and hilltop reveals. Do not copy its systemic physics or shrine structure.
- `Sable`: use calm low-poly silhouettes, sparse landmarks, desert haze, and readable negative space.
- `A Short Hike`: use compact objective chains where the next goal is near enough to understand but far enough to invite route choice.
- `Journey`: use dune crests, weather-framed horizons, and arrival moments with minimal UI.
- `Firewatch`: use color, weather, light direction, and distant landmark shapes to make navigation feel authored.
- `Valheim`: use biome identity and weather mood to make exploration legible. Do not add survival, crafting, or resource loops for this version.
- `Outer Wilds`: use curiosity-driven visible phenomena and objective breadcrumbs. Do not copy time loops or puzzle mechanics.

## Implementation Guidance

### Weather

- Model weather as deterministic cells or bands sampled from world coordinates and seed, then blend by player position.
- Drive existing systems first: scene fog color/range, sky gradient, cloud puff count or opacity, directional/hemisphere light colors, water tint, and optional environment accent colors.
- Keep particle weather optional and tiny. If rain, snow, or dust streaks are added, use one camera-relative instanced or line batch with strict caps and no per-drop collision.
- Weather should clarify objectives: rain can darken the sky and brighten water contrast, wind can animate clouds and water shimmer, and snow can increase contrast on rock and beacon silhouettes.

### Water

- Keep the recentered water plane as the default renderer path.
- Add shoreline readability before reflective realism in future passes: foam bands, wet sand tint, shallow-water color, and low-poly wave edge shapes.
- Treat player interaction as readable feedback, not simulation. In v1.2.0 the existing water-blocking counter remains the gameplay contract; future passes can add wake, foam, or sound feedback near the blocked point.
- If `Water.js` is tested, isolate it behind a feature flag or single implementation switch and compare calls, triangles, textures, render targets, FPS, and heap against the current plane.
- Do not add rivers or streamed fluid courses in v1.2.0. They need terrain carving, path continuity, and more collision rules than this version should carry.

### Objective Loop

- Start the player with one visible weather beacon or survey stone within 120 to 220 meters.
- On attune, select the next target from loaded or near-loaded deterministic candidates, preferring biome edges, shore shelves, ridges, and vista sites.
- Require three attunes for the first expedition completion state.
- Use world presentation first: beacon color, weather clearing, cloud break, light shaft, or low-poly signal shape. HUD text can confirm progress but must not be the only navigation method.
- Store no map. The current target can be recomputed from seed, start anchor, attune index, and target candidate grammar.

## Performance Guardrails

The existing hard contract remains:

- Visible-browser target: 60 FPS.
- Chromium headless smoke floor: greater than 45 FPS.
- JS heap: under 100 MB when `performance.memory` is available.
- Exploration render calls: under 160 after the objective, water normal map, and weather particle systems.
- Total triangles: under 190k for the default camera and terrain radius.
- Textures: under 14 after adding the procedural water normal texture.
- Chunk build time: under 12 ms in smoke.

v1.2.0 can spend a little more on terrain and environment density, but only inside those limits:

- Keep `TERRAIN_RADIUS = 4` and do not raise fog distance as a substitute for objective readability.
- Raise `CHUNK_SEGMENTS` from 20 to 24 for this minor release and verify the terrain geometry estimate stays above 2 MB but below 8 MB.
- Target total triangles under 170k, with 190k as the hard stop.
- Target render calls under 145, with 160 as the hard stop.
- Limit weather to 0 to 2 visible additional draw calls.
- Limit water upgrades to the existing water draw call plus one procedural normal texture.
- Cap renderer pixel ratio at 0.65 for this browser build so the denser world clears the 45 FPS headless smoke floor and targets 60 FPS in normal visible play.
- If `Water.js` is enabled, count its render target and normal map against the texture budget.
- Keep weather and objective placement rebuilds tied to chunk/weather-cell signature changes, not every frame.
- Add debug counters for weather type, weather blend, objective index, target distance, water effect count, weather draw calls if available, and estimated effect memory.

## Acceptance Tests

Before implementation handoff, v1.2.0 should prove:

- The default scene still reaches the budget gates.
- At least three weather states can be forced through debug controls or deterministic target locations.
- The first objective chain produces three reachable targets from the default spawn.
- Water-blocking still prevents entering unsafe water and reports feedback through debug stats.
- Rain and snow effects do not cause canvas blanking, text overlap, sorting artifacts, or player occlusion.
- Screenshots cover title, debug world view, biome terrain, shoreline, forest, rocky slope, rain, snow, and objective progress.
